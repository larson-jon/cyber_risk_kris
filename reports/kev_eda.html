Definitions

CVE — Common Vulnerabilities and Exposures

A standardized public identifier for a single known security vulnerability, written as CVE-YYYY-NNNNN (e.g. CVE-2021-44228). The program is run by MITRE and sponsored by CISA; each CVE names one specific flaw in software or hardware.

NIST's National Vulnerability Database (NVD) enriches every CVE with a severity score (CVSS), weakness type (CWE), affected products, and references. There are hundreds of thousands of CVEs, ranging from trivial to critical — and the large majority are never known to be exploited.

KEV — Known Exploited Vulnerabilities

CISA's authoritative catalog of the specific CVEs that have been confirmed exploited in the wild by attackers. It is a small, curated subset of all CVEs (~1,700 vs. hundreds of thousands). To be listed, CISA requires a CVE ID, reliable evidence of active exploitation, and a clear remediation.

Each KEV entry adds exploitation-focused fields a plain CVE lacks: the date CISA added it (date_added), a federal remediation due_date, and whether it is tied to a known ransomware campaign.

Relationship: every KEV entry is a CVE — but only the small fraction that attackers are actively using. In short: CVE = “a known vulnerability exists”; KEV = “this vulnerability is being exploited right now — patch it urgently.” This report joins the two: KEV tells us what is being exploited, NVD/CVE tells us how severe it is and what it affects.

Has the median been getting lower? At the monthly level the median is noisy (swinging between single digits and 200+ days), so a straight-line fit across all months is essentially flat (slope -0.28 days/month). At the annual level, though, the median has dropped — 2025: 26 days; 2026: 15 days. So the honest read is: no clean monthly downtrend, but a real year-over-year decline in typical time-to-exploitation. The mean runs far above the median because a handful of very old CVEs get newly exploited each month, so median is the better indicator. The Zero-day (≤0) column counts records whose time-to-exploit is 0 or negative — CISA added the CVE to KEV on or before the day NVD published it, indicating exploitation at (or before) public disclosure.
